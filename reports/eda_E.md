**Worksheet evidence:**
- **D10:** E5. In both datasets, τ = 10 is where recurrence crosses 90%.
- **D13, D14:** E2. A boolean flag works (4.3% and 4.6% ambiguous). Exclude the optic lobes by default for whole-brain claims.
- **D15:** E1. The tie rule affects 20–25% of central neurons.
- **D17:** E1's worked examples.
- **Any sex claim:** E6. On the same types, male–female recurrence matches the female–female baseline at τ = 10 (89.7% vs 89.5%), so an edge-level sex difference needs more than recurrence alone.

**Choices made here, to confirm with the group:**
- The **malecns nerve-cord list** (34 neuron regions plus the nerves that appear only in edgeroi; `CRN`, 16 neurons and 8 synapses, is included).
- **E7 columnar neurons.** A columnar type is a fafb CX type with at least one neuron labelled `columnar`, and whole types are taken in both datasets: by `cell_type` in fafb and by `flywire_type` in malecns, which has no `cell_sub_class`. Whole types matter because fafb labels only 4 of its 244 PFNp neurons `columnar`. Selecting by label (as the first version did) kept 979 fafb neurons; by type it keeps 1,224, against 1,311 in malecns (PFNp 244 vs 290, PFR_a 1 vs 28, FS3 38 vs 60 in the CX).
- **E4 inputs** are counted from all Traced partners (see the table).
- **E6 matching.** Compound type values (fafb `hemibrain_type` such as `AVLP386,AVLP388`, malecns `flywire_type` such as `LAL074,LAL084`) are merged into match groups, so a type that maps to several on the other side is compared as one unit. Merged groups (such as VS1–VS8, or a malecns `flywire_type` listing ten candidates) hold 2.3% (malecns matching) and 11% (hemibrain matching) of keyed fafb neurons; dropping them moves the 10–19 share by at most 0.3 points. Hemibrain side comes from the last `_R`/`_L` token of the instance (for central-complex columnar types, the protocerebral-bridge half, taken as the soma side), else from the region's side; 630 neurons stay unknown and are left out (598 in FB).

**What surprised me:**
- One-synapse type edges recur less often within FlyWire (32%) than Schlegel et al. found between FlyWire and the hemibrain (42%). malecns gives the same 32%, so this is not a FlyWire quirk. **Checked:** it is a population-mix effect. Recurrence rises with type size (smaller side count of the two types). In fafb it is 31% at 1, 35% at 2–4, 42% at 5–19 and 56% at 20+. Singleton-type pairs make up 80% of central type pairs, so they set the headline. Restricting the type set moves the figure by about 0.01 at most (`e5_restricted`). Type pairs where a type is missing on one side cannot recur: 23,212 in fafb and 60,269 in malecns. The 10+ figures agree with the paper.
- The two central-complex rosters disagree in both datasets. By cell class CX there are 2,875 (fafb) and 2,950 (malecns) neurons. By region there are 2,617 and 2,657, and 2,560 and 2,628 are in both. In fafb, 315 CX-class neurons have most of their synapses outside the CX (for example in SMP and CRE), and 57 neurons whose region is CX belong to another class or none (TuBu 50, unclassified 6). Project 5 keeps the annotation-based roster and states why.
- malecns has about twice fafb's central synapses at τ = 10 (28.3M vs 13.4M) with a similar neuron count. Raw counts are not comparable between the datasets; compare shares, or thresholds relative to each neuron's input.
